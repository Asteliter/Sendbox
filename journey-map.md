# CJM — Signing a lease you don't fully understand

**JTBD:** When I'm about to sign a legal document I don't fully understand and can't justify hiring a lawyer for, I want to quickly see which specific clauses are unusual or risky and what to double-check, so I can sign with informed confidence instead of blind hope.

| | Receiving the document | Skimming familiar terms | Hitting confusing clauses | Searching for answers | Deciding to sign anyway |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Actions** | Landlord's lawyer sends over the 14-page lease ahead of signing. | Skims for rent amount and move-in date — looks fine, keeps going. | Rereads the "Renewal" paragraph three times; a few pages later reads the "Liability" section on damage "regardless of cause." | Googles a couple of the exact phrases from the lease. | Weighs pushing back vs. looking difficult; signs, telling herself she'll deal with it if it ever becomes a problem. |
| **Touchpoints** | 🔒 The lease document itself (email/PDF) — the point where her personal and financial details first enter the picture. | The document pages, read directly. | The document pages, read directly — no outside help available in the moment. | Google search → generic law-firm blog posts. | Signing the lease. |
| **Thoughts & Emotions** | 😐 mild apprehension — "dense legal language, this is a lot to get through." | 🙂 mild relief — the basics she checked look normal. | 😟→😠 confusion tipping into frustration — specific trigger: rereads "automatically renewing... unless written notice is provided no less than ninety (90) days" three times and still can't tell what happens if she misses the deadline; then unease reading "regardless of cause," wondering if she's liable for damage that isn't her fault. | 😞 discouraged — the search results are generic and don't confirm whether *her* document/situation is normal. | 😞 resigned anxiety — unresolved uncertainty about renewal/liability, weighed against not wanting to seem "difficult" by questioning the landlord's lawyer. |
| **Pain Points & Opportunities** | How might we help someone see, before they start reading, how much of a document actually needs careful attention? | How might we confirm that the parts a user already checked really are ordinary, so they don't feel they have to re-read everything out of anxiety? | How might we translate a specific unusual clause into a plain-language flag the moment someone gets stuck on it, without saying whether it's legally enforceable? | How might we let someone check whether a specific clause in front of them is common or unusual, instead of a generic article that knows nothing about their document? | How might we give someone enough clarity before signing to decide, on their own, whether a clause is worth a second opinion — without needing to feel "difficult" to get it? |
| **AI touch?** | Caution — AI can segment the document into clauses for structure; pure preprocessing, no interpretation yet, low risk. | Yes, low risk — confirming a clause matches a common/expected pattern is comparative, not a legal verdict. | Yes, high caution — this is the core value moment: AI describes and compares against a reference pattern, but must never assert enforceability or state "you're locked in" as fact. | Yes, medium — grounded comparison against a reference clause library beats generic search, but same no-verdict limit applies. | Caution — AI should not tell her whether to sign; it can only keep the unusual points visible and flag when professional review is worth it. |

**Redesign target:** *Hitting confusing clauses* — the confusion-to-frustration phase (😟→😠) is the worst-emotion moment and the one this project exists to fix. Priority HMW: **How might we translate a specific unusual clause into a plain-language flag the moment someone gets stuck on it, without saying whether it's legally enforceable?**

**Assumptions:**
- Assumed the lease arrived as an email/PDF attachment — the story only says "sent over," not the exact channel.
- Assumed the "Renewal" and "Liability" reads happen in the same sitting, not separate visits to the document.
- Assumed a graded emotional arc (neutral → mild relief → confusion/frustration → discouragement → resigned anxiety) rather than one flat "frustrated" — inferred from the narrative's specific details (rereading three times, googling, not wanting to seem difficult), not stated directly as emotions in the story.
- Assumed "regardless of cause" reads as worrying-but-not-panicked, based on the story's overall measured tone.
